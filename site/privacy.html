Haunt privacy policy

Haunt: Fake GPS Location · Effective 13 October 2026

In short: Haunt has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no crash reporting. It never sends your real location anywhere. To search places, plan routes and draw the map it talks to public map services, which receive the coordinates you work with and your IP address. Everything else stays on your phone.

Who we are

Haunt is a free, open-source app (GPL-3.0) developed by crockalet. The source code is at github.com/crockalet/haunt, so you can check everything this page says. We do not run any servers of our own.

Your real location

Haunt asks for location permission so it can show where you really are on the map and start a fake location from there. Your real location is only used on your device. Haunt does not send it, store it on a server, or share it.

Data that leaves your phone

Some features send requests directly from your phone to third-party services. Haunt adds no identifier to these requests; like any internet request they include your IP address and an app identifier (Haunt/<version>).

  • Place search (Photon, run by komoot): the text you type and the coordinates of the area you're looking at, so nearby results come first. Looking up the address of a point sends that point's coordinates.
  • Routes (FOSSGIS routing for walking and cycling, the OSRM demo server for driving): the start, stop and end points of the route.
  • Map (OpenFreeMap): map tiles and fonts for the area shown on screen.

These are the default services. You can point Haunt at other servers in Settings; requests then go to the servers you choose. Each service handles requests under its own privacy policy. The coordinates sent are normally the places you are faking, but if you search or route near where you really are, they can reveal that area.

Data stored on your phone

Favourites, recent places, imported tracks (GPX/KML), settings and the activity log are stored only in Haunt's app storage. They are not sent anywhere. Android may include them in your device backup if you have backups turned on. Uninstalling Haunt or clearing its storage deletes them.

Google Play services

The version of Haunt from Google Play uses Google Play services to set the fake location for apps that use Google's location APIs. Google Play services is part of your device and handles data under Google's privacy policy.

Control over ADB

Haunt can be controlled from a computer connected through Android Debug Bridge (ADB). That connection stays between your phone and the computer you authorised. Haunt does not open any network port.

Children

Haunt is a tool for developers and testers and is not directed at children.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update this page and its effective date. The full history is in the source repository.
